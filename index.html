Not about size

Rome ranks 2nd with 3 rail connections. Buffalo has 10 and ranks 3rd. The bottlenecks are the small towns.

Why these towns

Rome is the Oneida Carry — boats hauled overland between the Mohawk and Wood Creek, the only significant water route from the Atlantic to the interior other than the St. Lawrence. Little Falls is the gorge, a 44-foot drop over about a mile. Both were canalised in the 1790s, a generation before the Erie.

200 years on, the same geography.

How the score is counted

Small print

  • Ranks 5–8 (.121 .119 .112 .109) are within noise. The claim is the set, not the order.
  • The towns are New York’s incorporated cities outside the downstate counties — all 53 of them, of which 35 have a vertex on the rail network. They were not picked along a rail line. 12 of the 35 sit on the Erie Canal main line.
  • Canal cities are over-represented among those 35: 12 of the 13 on the main line have a rail vertex, against 23 of the other 40. Only Cohoes drops out.
  • A junction belongs to a town within 10 km of its boundary. At strict city limits Rochester scores exactly zero — the shortest Buffalo–Syracuse route takes the CSX West Shore Subdivision, passing about 2 km south of the boundary, rather than the Rochester Subdivision through the city. 232.5 km against 235.3.
  • Present-day OpenStreetMap track; shortest paths weighted by length, so this is geometry, not timetables.
